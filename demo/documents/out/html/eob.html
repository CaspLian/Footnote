If you disagree with how we processed this claim

What if I need help understanding this statement?

Call Member Services at (614) 555-0186 (TTY 711), Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–8 p.m. ET. We can explain any decision on this statement, including the codes on page 2.

What if I don’t agree with a decision?

You have the right to appeal any decision not to pay for an item or service, in whole or in part. You must file your appeal within 180 days after you receive this statement.

How do I file an appeal?

Write to us at Pellridge Health Plan, Attn: Member Appeals, PO Box 30425, Merrowfield, OH 43617-0425, or fax (614) 555-0188. Include your name, member ID, the claim number (2626404188170), the service you disagree with and why. You may send letters from your doctor, bills, medical records or other information that supports your appeal.

Who may file an appeal?

You, or someone you name to act for you (an authorized representative). Call us for the form to name a representative.

What if my situation is urgent?

If waiting could seriously harm your health, ask for an expedited appeal. We will decide within 72 hours. You may ask for an external review at the same time.

Can I get copies of information about my claim?

Yes, free of charge, including the claim documents, the plan rules we used, and the diagnosis and treatment codes and their meanings. If you think a coding error caused a denial, ask for these codes.

What happens next?

We will review your appeal and send you a written decision. If we still deny payment, or do not decide in time, you may ask for an external review by an independent review organization. You must ask within four months after you receive our final decision. The independent reviewer’s decision is binding on the plan. If your plan is an employer plan covered by ERISA, you also have the right to bring a civil action under section 502(a) of ERISA after we decide your appeal.

Protection from surprise medical bills

When you get emergency care, or are treated by an out-of-network provider at an in-network hospital or ambulatory surgical center, you are protected from balance billing: you should not be charged more than your plan’s copayment, coinsurance and deductible, and what you pay counts toward your in-network deductible and out-of-pocket maximum. If you believe you have been wrongly billed, call Member Services or the federal No Surprises Help Desk.

Other help with your rights

For help with your rights or with filing an appeal, you may also contact your state’s department of insurance or consumer assistance program, or, for an employer group health plan, the U.S. Department of Labor’s Employee Benefits Security Administration.
