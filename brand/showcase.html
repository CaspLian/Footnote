The Footnote identity
Footnote

Medical bills hide their truth in fine print. The asterisk is the oldest promise in print that an explanation exists. This identity reclaims it for the patient: the mark sits beside the charge worth asking about, and the app is the footnote that finally gets read.

Five capsule arms and an open center make the printers’ asterisk recognizable at every size.

Scales to 16 px without losing the asterisk read. Dark icon uses the palette's accent-soft sage, not a new color.

A

Brand analysis

The app photographs a medical bill or EOB, has the patient verify every extracted value, compares confirmed codes against Medicare benchmarks, explains everything in plain or detailed language with spoken narration, and prepares correction and review requests the patient sends themselves. Family profiles, deadlines, and records stay on the device. No account.

Its voice is fixed in the product: "Understand the bill. Own the next step." and "A little clarity is on its way." Its ethics are fixed in the domain rules: findings are questions, never proof of an error, and never a savings promise.

The audience includes people with limited English, low literacy, and low vision. Spanish copy ships in the code. Everything about the identity must stay calm, legible, and honest.

B · Footnote Mark

Finalist
Idea
A printers' asterisk: the oldest mark meaning "there is an explanation for this." The app puts it next to the charge worth asking about.
Construction
Five capsule arms, 72° rotation, open 13-unit center. Capsule radius echoes the app's pill buttons; the open center is the rounded negative space the skill prescribes.
Color
Accent green on paper; accent-soft sage on ink. Single shape, so one color carries it anywhere.
At 16 px
The strongest survivor of the set; still clearly an asterisk.
Honest read
Lives near the overused star and sparkle family. It must stay typographic: capsule arms, hollow center, never four points, never a gradient. Drawn this way it reads print, not AI badge.

D · The Caret

Finalist
Idea
The proofreader's insertion mark rising into a gap in the bill's line: something was left out here, ask about it. It is also an arrow taking the next step up through the paperwork.
Construction
Two unequal line segments (30 and 26 units) with a 16-unit gap; caret apex optically centered in the gap. One stroke weight, 7, throughout.
Color
Ink line, accent caret. Dark variant: warm-white line, sage caret.
At 16 px
Reads cleanly as a mark under a line; the green loses punch at the smallest size.
Honest read
The most original concept here and completely unclaimed, but it needs one sentence of telling before the metaphor lands, and it is the quieter store thumbnail.

E · Sheet & Note

Eliminated

The app's loader distilled: honest continuity, strong on dark, and exactly why it fails. A dog-eared document with text lines is the stock notes glyph of a thousand icon libraries. Kept as an in-product illustration, not as the brand.

F · Pleat

Eliminated

Accordion folds opening upward. Pleasant, scalable, and anonymous: stacked chevrons belong to rank badges and fast-forward buttons. No amount of color makes them ownable.

E

The Footnote identity

Footnote, marked by the printers' asterisk. One symbol carries the name, the feature, and the promise: the fine print on a medical bill finally gets an explanation, and the patient gets the next step. The caret stays in the system as the runner-up and as an in-product mark; the folded paper stays in the loader, where it already lives.

  • The name and asterisk connect the identity to the explanation beside each charge.
  • The asterisk must always be drawn as built here: five capsule arms, open center, flat brand color. No four-point sparkles, no gradients, no rotation.
  • Footnote names the app, the analysis service and the voice helper. The supplied asterisk marks the app icon and headers.
